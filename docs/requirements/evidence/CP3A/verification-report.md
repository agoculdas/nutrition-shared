Scope: India dietary preferences, ingredient metadata, derived preset metadata, household-unit metadata, provenance validation, and integration with existing food selection and meal prep. Shared edition retains its defaults and food catalog. All CP3A exit requirements now have passing local evidence; formal checkpoint acceptance remains a stakeholder decision.

The sidecar covers 167 components and 314 preset contexts. Every component has a row-level qualified nutrition record in `food-source-register.json`; the strict release validator reports zero errors, warnings, or unapproved records. India applies the qualified names, macros, and controlled conversions through the `qualifiedFoodCatalog` edition flag. Shared keeps the inherited catalog values. Household units are controlled by `portion-qualification.json`, using NIST metric-kitchen definitions, named USDA FoodData Central portions, and an explicitly application-defined 250 ml glass.

Initial browser review found and returned three defects for correction: vegan meal prep incorrectly returned no recipes; preset descriptions could mention garlic omitted from their macro composition; opening a historical composition in Builder could remove incompatible ingredients. Static day-guidance examples also needed to follow the preference. These findings demonstrate why structural tests alone are insufficient for this change.

Final automated verification after nutrition qualification: `npm test` passed 80 tests, catalog and requirement validation, production build, Shared regression, and clean edition-change verification. `final-verification.json` records the final command outcomes; `npm-test.txt` is the earlier portion-stage run. The food-model suites cover Shared preservation, opt-in India overrides with stable IDs, source-backed production conversions, dietary derivation, uncertain qualification precedence, and vegan meal-prep saved setup/reopen/log snapshots, including a conflicting animal ingredient.

The coordinator separately evaluated all eight combinations of the four diet preferences and the onion/garlic toggle across all 314 presets. No returned item was incompatible with its active preference. `filter-audit.json` records the counts. `source-candidate-audit.json` contains ranked candidates, while the reviewed policy in `food-source-policy.json` corrects false token matches and documents transparent substitutions where the selected USDA releases have no exact food. `food-source-register.json` records the final food description, FDC identifier, portion basis, macros, selection method, and source URL for every row. Creatine is the sole non-food entry and is explicitly labeled as an application-controlled supplement whose energy is not represented, with an NIH public-domain source. Strict release validation passes with 167 qualified nutrition records and zero unapproved nutrition or unit records. `CNT-008` is Pass.

Portion qualification corrects two inherited conversion assumptions in the India edition: cooked white rice changes from 200 g to the FDC 168935 value of 158 g per cup, and one cup of sambar changes from 1.5 to two controlled 124 g katori servings using FDC 2707430. Ghee uses 14 g per FDC tablespoon divided into three NIST metric-kitchen teaspoons. Qualified India labels identify the exact source foods; Shared labels and conversions remain unchanged. Three production-fixture tests verify all eight named units, their approved provenance and implementation within 0.1%. `CNT-005` is Pass.

Final browser demonstration on the local India build: vegan mode retained exactly the chickpea curry and lentil pasta recipes; opening curry showed its scaled ingredients, coverage and collapsed **View instructions** disclosure. Vegan preference survived reload. Adding onion/garlic exclusion returned no batch recipes, as expected for this collection. Chicken search returned zero results, and Builder showed plant proteins and legumes. Empty-day examples followed the preference. Original All foods / onion-garlic allowed settings were restored, with no meals logged or existing targets changed during this check.

The historical-edit correction was inspected in source: opening a Builder seed now copies all original rows rather than filtering them. Existing logs are not rewritten on preference changes. Saved composition filtering derives missing dietary metadata. The 25 preset qualification overlays add two explicit onion/garlic positives and 23 conservative unresolved flags; a known positive component takes precedence. Coconut milk's authored dietary metadata was corrected to vegan; macro values remain unchanged.

Disposition: all nine CP3A requirements pass local verification. The checkpoint is ready for stakeholder acceptance and progression to CP3B. The qualified catalog records exact USDA/FNDDS descriptions as `sourceDescription` for provenance only; display names remain the application labels (stakeholder review on 2026-09-06 rejected USDA descriptions as user-facing names). Documented substitutions for unsupported generic/composite labels remain; the catalog does not establish restaurant-specific accuracy. A follow-up macro-basis review of the qualified rows is recorded separately in `macro-remediation.md`. Recipe cooking qualification, installed offline behavior, physical-device testing, and full accessibility acceptance retain their later-checkpoint status. The browser demonstration does not replace those campaigns.
